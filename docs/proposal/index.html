BusPulse Delhi

Bus times from real buses on the road. Transport Stack Hackathon 2026 · Phase 1 · Problem 1: real-time bus traffic model and journey planner (with Problem 2's crowding as a layer) · Team: Farhan

The problem

Delhi's bus timetable assumes fixed travel times. Real times change with the hour, the day and the road, so a rider cannot tell whether a trip will take 30 minutes or 60, how long the wait will be, or whether the bus will be full. Thousands of buses already report their position every ~10 seconds. BusPulse uses this GPS stream to measure travel times and turns them into journey times a rider can rely on.

What we built

  • A travel-time model from 31 days of bus GPS (913 M position fixes): fixes are cleaned (duplicates, GPS jumps, data gaps), snapped to stop clusters, and turned into 48 M stop-to-stop observations. Each stretch then gets a running time and a stop dwell time for every hour of weekdays, Saturdays and Sundays. Where data is thin, a 4-level fallback steps down to the timetable.
  • A Google-Maps-like API: GET /journeys?from=…&to=…&depart=… takes stop names, addresses or coordinates and returns 2–3 options. Each option has the walk, boarding stop, route, wait, ride, changes, alighting stop, total time and arrival, plus an honest range ("on 9 of 10 days you arrive by…"), the same trip at a quieter hour, and how full the bus usually is. Also /matrix, /traffic, /live/vehicles, /places/search and interactive docs at /docs.
  • Real waits: measured from real bus arrivals rather than the timetable. On thinly served routes, real waits are about 6× the timetable's promise, and the planner steers riders to routes that actually run.
  • Live data: a GTFS-Realtime connector (built-in decoder) is running on the Transport Stack live feed. It tracked 5,424 buses on 24 Nov 2026 and uses the same measurement code as the historical model. The last 15 minutes of live runs are blended with history and fade out further along the trip.
  • A simple web app for every rider: big numbers, English/हिंदी, a map with your route, live buses (yours near your stop, ride-hailing style), a 3D view, city traffic by hour, crowding, an in-app Accuracy page, and a phone layout.

Design choices that matter

  • Waiting is part of travel time, modelled separately. Stop dwell is kept apart from running time; signal delay stays inside running time; the rider's wait comes from real arrivals. The app shows "1 h 9 min on the bus · 30 min waiting".
  • Honest ranges, checked: the "9 of 10 days" promise holds 89.9 % of the time.
  • Little or no live data: pure history, then fallbacks; the app shows the share of each trip measured from real buses.
  • Noisy GPS: 38.5 % duplicates removed, GPS jumps dropped, gaps over 3 min never produce a run.
  • Same code for live and history: the live connector reuses the pipeline's stop-visit logic. On a replayed feed it matches the offline numbers within 1 %.
  • Built to run cheaply: about 0.5 s per full answer, under 400 MB RAM, a 50 MB model bundle, one command to start.

Who it is for

Delhi's buses carry about 41 lakh trips a day (Economic Survey of Delhi 2023-24), with buses running about 81 % full on average. In a 2024 Greenpeace survey, 87 % of women bus riders said they wait more than 10 minutes and only 21 % said buses come on time (more in docs/USER_RESEARCH.md). Our users are daily bus riders who need to know when to leave and whether they will get a seat, in Hindi or English, on a basic phone. Also planners and operators: city traffic by hour, the slowest stretches, and travel-time matrices.
